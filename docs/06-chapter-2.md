## **2.1. Competitors**

**OnTrack School**

OnTrack School is a platform developed in Latin America that helps manage school transportation in a safer and more organized way. It allows parents to view the school vehicle's location in real time, while schools and companies can monitor student attendance. This makes it a useful tool for improving communication and service tracking (OnTrack, n.d.).

**titiGO**

titiGO is a Peruvian platform designed for tracking and controlling school transportation. Its proposal allows families to receive real-time notifications about each student's trip status, recording when they board the vehicle, arrive at school, or return home. In addition, it uses an exit control system through a unique QR code, seeking to provide complete security in communication and trust for parents (titiGO, 2026).

**BatOnRoute**

BatOnRoute is European-origin software that helps manage school transportation routes. It allows schools and companies to know the location of vehicles in real time, keep attendance control, and send notifications to parents when the school vehicle is nearby. Its focus is on improving service organization and providing greater security to everyone involved (BatOnRoute, n.d.).

### **2.1.2. Strategies and Tactics Against Competitors**

In a city like Metropolitan Lima, where traffic and transportation disorder are part of everyday life, at KidsOnWay we seek to differentiate ourselves by offering a simpler and more useful solution for parents and drivers compared to other applications in the sector. We do not only focus on real-time location, but also on improving safety and peace of mind throughout the entire route.

**Strengths**

* **Alert automation**

  Unlike current methods such as calls or WhatsApp messages, the application sends automatic notifications when the school vehicle is nearby or arrives at the destination. This allows the driver to avoid using the phone while driving and focus on the route.

* **Clear and reliable information for parents**

  Parents can view the route without needing to call or write messages. This gives them greater peace of mind and confidence during their children's transportation. In addition, they can access real-time information.

**Weaknesses**

* **Low market presence**

  Since we are a new startup, we still do not have a well-known brand. To build trust, pilot tests will be carried out with some users and their experiences will be shared to demonstrate that our solution is solid and worth adopting.

* **Internet dependency**

  In some areas of Lima, the signal may fail, which affects real-time updates. To reduce this problem, the app will be optimized to consume little data and store information when there is no signal.

**Opportunities**

* **Traffic and safety problems**

  Traffic in Lima makes travel times unpredictable. The app can help parents know when the school vehicle will arrive and reduce waiting time on the street.

* **Greater use of technology**

  More and more services are becoming digitalized, which allows solutions such as KidsOnWay to be better accepted by drivers and transportation companies.

**Threats**

* **Competition from other platforms**

  There are more developed applications in other countries. In response, KidsOnWay will focus on better adapting to the local reality, with accessible prices and solutions closer to the local context.

* **Resistance to change**

  Some drivers prefer to continue using traditional methods. For this reason, the application will be simple to use and will provide a clear explanation of its benefits.

## **2.2. Interviews**

### **2.2.1. Interview Design**

In order to obtain qualitative information that helps us validate our ideas and better understand users, three interviews were designed for each target group of the KidsOnWay platform. The questions were planned to obtain open-ended answers, avoiding yes-or-no responses as much as possible. In addition, they were organized into blocks to learn about users' profiles, technology habits, the problems they face, and what they expect from a school transportation service.

**Segment #1: Independent Drivers**

They introduce themselves, ask for consent to interview the participant, and begin:

1. Could you tell me your age, district of residence, and how many years you have been working as a school transportation driver?
2. What brand and model of vehicle do you currently drive? Is it owned by you or rented?
3. In your daily routine, what applications do you use most on your phone? Some examples are Waze, WhatsApp, or social networks.
4. How do you organize the order of your route every morning? Do you use any digital tool or rely on your memory?
5. Describe your experience driving during rush hour in Lima. How does traffic affect your mood and punctuality?
6. What bothers you the most when you arrive to pick up a student and they are not ready at the door?
7. How many times a day do you receive calls or messages from parents asking about your location while you are driving?
8. Have you ever had a dangerous distraction because you tried to answer your phone to notify someone that you were already near a home?
9. If an application automatically notified parents when you were 5 minutes away from their home without you doing anything, how would your workday change?
10. How important is it to you that the application is extremely simple to use, for example with large buttons and without distracting you from driving?
11. What economic or time-related benefit would you expect to obtain from using a platform like ours?

**Segment #2: Companies Dedicated to School Transportation**

They introduce themselves, ask for consent to interview the participant, and begin:

1. What is the name of the company, how many units are in your current fleet, and in which districts of Lima do you mainly operate?
2. What is your position within the company and what are your main daily logistical challenges?
3. What methods do you currently use to supervise whether your drivers are following the established routes and schedules?
4. How do you handle student attendance records? For example, is it manual, on paper, or do you have a digital system?
5. What is the highest operating cost you face, such as fuel, maintenance, or fines, and how do you try to reduce it?
6. What type of complaints do you receive most frequently from parents?
7. How valuable would it be for your company to have a centralized dashboard where you can see all your units on a single map in real time?
8. How do you think offering parents an application to monitor their children's bus would impact their trust?
9. To adopt a solution like KidsOnWay, what type of reports or statistical data would you need the platform to provide monthly?

**Interview Summary**

Dery Estrella Perez is the owner and driver of a small family school transportation company with a fleet of five units, operating mainly in Los Olivos and San Martín de Porres. In her daily routine, she faces challenges such as avoiding areas with constant roadwork and maintaining punctuality when picking up and dropping off students. Since it is a small team, coordination is quite direct, relying on calls between drivers to report delays or route problems.

Unit supervision is completely manual and depends on constant communication among the team. Likewise, attendance records are kept in individual notebooks by each driver, which creates a poorly centralized system that is prone to errors or loss of information. Although this way of working is functional, it limits the ability to have a general real-time view of the service.

Regarding costs, the main problems are related to fuel consumption and fines, which they try to reduce by looking for alternative routes to avoid traffic. From the customer service side, the main point of tension is parents' uncertainty, since they frequently call when they perceive delays, generating additional pressure on drivers while they are behind the wheel.

Dery sees high value in having a more organized system, such as a centralized dashboard that allows her to see the location of her units and react quickly to unforeseen events, such as mechanical failures. She also believes that an app for parents would provide peace of mind and improve the perceived safety of the service. She would expect our solution to include clear reports on route incidents and monthly attendance, helping her maintain better operational and administrative control.

## **2.3. Needfinding**

### **2.3.1. User Persona**

The User Personas have been built as representations of the main types of KidsOnWay users. These profiles are directly based on the findings obtained during the interviews, where recurring problems were identified, such as stress, difficulty managing several tasks at the same time, and the risks of using a phone while driving or coordinating a route.

In addition, the competitor analysis was considered, which made it possible to understand that users are looking for simpler and more automated solutions than the current options in the market. In this context, the need was identified for a tool that reduces manual intervention and facilitates use in real situations.

**Segment 1: Independent Drivers**

This archetype represents drivers who own their own vehicles and operate independently. Their main motivation is to provide a safe service and maintain a good reputation, but they are constantly frustrated by the stress of Lima traffic and phone interruptions from parents. They value technology as long as it offers automatic and extremely simple tools that allow them to keep their full attention on driving.

## **2.5. Ubiquitous Language**

Ubiquitous Language is a common and standardized vocabulary shared by the development team and domain experts, who in this case are school transportation drivers, administrators, and parents. It is a type of glossary that ensures all stakeholders share the same understanding of the key business concepts, reducing ambiguity in requirements specification and source code.

* **Administrator** is the user responsible for the logistical management of the fleet. Their main role is to monitor routes, resolve incidents, and manage high-level communication with customers.
* **Proximity Alert** is the automated push notification generated by the system and sent to the guardian's mobile device when the unit is at a predetermined distance or time from the pickup point.
* **Parent** is the final customer who hires the school transportation service and requires visibility over the transportation status of their minor child.
* **Attendance** is the record of the exact moment when a student boards or gets off the vehicle.
* **Driver** is the user responsible for driving the vehicle, executing the established route, and ensuring the physical safety of students during the trip.
* **Dashboard** is the centralized web or tablet interface, designed for administrators and parents, that shows the real-time location, speed, and status of all active units through GPS integration.
* **Student** is the passenger who will be transported.
* **Fleet** is the total set of operational vehicles managed by the same school transportation company.
* **Route** is the planned path of pickup and drop-off points.
* **Unit** is the physical transportation vehicle assigned to a driver and to a specific route.
